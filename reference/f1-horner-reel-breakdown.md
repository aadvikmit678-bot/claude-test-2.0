# Reference breakdown: Metro Media House — "Behind every Verstappen is a Horner"

Source: Instagram reel by @metromedia.house (Metro Media House), posted 2026-08-15.
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcEN0yGh4DD/

This is a style reference for building new edits in the same editing language. Everything here
was measured from the file: frames at 15 fps (first 15 s) and 10 fps (the rest), full-resolution
stills, per-frame scene scores, colour sampling, a spectrogram, onset detection and word-level
transcription. Times are in seconds from the start.

---

## 5. Typography

### Main white captions (the "glow" type)
- **Typeface**: Helvetica Neue **Bold** (or a close match: Neue Haas Grotesk Display Bold, Inter Display Bold). Tight neo-grotesk, double-storey "a", flat-cut "t".
- **Colour**: measured **#F7F4F7**, a very slightly warm off-white rather than pure white.
- **Tracking**: tight, about −3 % to −5 %; letters almost touch at large sizes.
- **Case**: as spoken, mostly lower case. Some brand names are deliberately lower-cased to echo their old logos or for tone (**facebook**, **myspace**, **saturday**, **horner**). Others keep capitals (Oracle, Stripe, Airbnb, Verstappen, Michael Schumacher).
- **Shadow**: soft dark drop shadow (about 30–40 % black, blur about 8 px, offset about 3 px down). Invisible on dark footage, visible on light backgrounds (for example "Max Verstappen" over a white sky).
- **Where the "glow" comes from**: there is **no permanent outer glow**. Measured background brightness right next to settled text matches the background further away, within 1–3 levels. The glowing look comes from the **reveal**:
  - **Frame 0**: the word appears already large (about 108–112 %), at about 50 % opacity, with a heavy Gaussian blur of roughly 20–30 px. It reads as a soft white bloom.
  - **Frames 1–6**: blur falls to 0, scale settles to 100 % and opacity rises to 100 %, with a strong ease-out.
  - **Total**: about 7 frames (0.23 s). The blur starts 2–9 frames **before** the spoken word, so the word is sharp exactly as it is heard.
  - **Multi-word phrases**: the second word blurs in after the first, left to right ("Max **Verstappen**", "best **driver**", "social **networks**", "Over**take**").
  - **Exit**: usually a hard cut to the next word. Occasionally a blur-out ("infront", "gap", "luck").

### Size hierarchy
| Role | Approximate size at 718 px frame height | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Function words | cap height ≈ 25–35 px (≈ 4–5 % of height) | the, at, on, you, is |
| Normal words | ≈ 40–55 px | Max Verstappen, telling |
| Emphasis words | x-height plus caps ≈ 120–150 px (≈ 17–21 %) | **speed**, **startup**, **facebook**, **alive** |
| Counters | ≈ 180 px (≈ 25 %) | **188mph**, **2016**, **130R** |
| Chapter numerals | ≈ 320 px (≈ 45 %) | **1**, **2**, **3** |
| Radio captions | ≈ 22 px cap height | *so we call that a pole?* |

### Lockups (repeating layouts)
1. **Small over big**: modifier words small, stacked tightly above the big keyword and aligned to it. Examples: "is the / **best driver**", "the / **fastest**", "He's not / **always**", "but he / **wins**", "driver / **alive**".
2. **Big over small**: big keyword with a small sub-line underneath. Examples: "**Verstappen** / is a Horner", "**facebook** / did · exactly · that" (sub-line words spread across the width), and every chapter title ("**1** / Overtake", "**2** / pole position", "**3** / slipstream").
3. **Two-line name**: "**Fernando** / **Alonso**" with negative leading (the lines nearly touch).
4. **Placement**: centred by default, but moved into empty space when a face or car fills the centre. Examples: "He's" on the right of the Baku photo, "anyway" on the right of the podium shot, "companies" on the left over Eisenberg.
5. **Depth (once)**: "driver alive" sits **behind** Schumacher. The subject was cut out and layered over the text.

### Black captions on paper
- Same font, near-black (#0A0A0A), centred, smaller than white captions (cap height about 25 px). Punch words are larger ("impossible", "everything", "luck").
- **Animation**: hard cut in at about 114 % scale, then eases down to 100 % over about 0.4 s. No blur.

### Numbers and counters
- Chapter numerals **1 / 2 / 3**: huge white Helvetica numerals with a **vertical alpha gradient**, solid at the top and fading to transparent at the bottom.
- **Rolling counters** (three in the video), always landing exactly on the spoken number with exponential ease-out (the steps get smaller near the end):
  - Speed: **147 → 200mph**.
  - Year: **2026 → 2005**, counting down.
  - Corner: **0R → 130R**.

### Special case: team-radio shout
- "**AAAAHH / \*\*\*\*\*\* / SAKE!!!**" in **electric blue #1C59F7**, solid.
- An ultra-wide, very heavy display face with rounded tops on the A's (Unbounded Black or Formula1 Display Wide family).
- Three stacked lines, centred in the upper half, over a magenta-washed onboard.
- The asterisks are chunky 8-lobed blobs that pop in one by one.

## 7. Colour and look

- **Footage grade**: lifted, milky blacks. The median darkest 1 % of each frame sits at 21/255, and 70 % of frames never reach true black. Highlights are rolled off (99th percentile ≈ 221). Saturation is moderate (0.26 average).
  - The grade puts broadcast TV, film stills, interviews, phone UI and 2005 standard-definition footage under one soft "matte" look so they feel like one piece.
- **Paper background**: warm bone off-white **#EEE4E0** in the centre, with a soft oval vignette down to **#E0D7D4** at the edges and a faint 2–3 px magenta fringe on the right edge.
  - It's used as a full-screen "breath" and as a desk surface for photo cards.
- **Accent colours**: none designed, except the one-off electric blue #1C59F7. All colour comes from the footage: Red Bull navy and red, Ferrari red, Aston green, Mercedes teal.
- **Low-key portraits**: Max (18.5) and Alonso (32.0) on near-black with hard side light and a prism light leak, used for introducing a person.

## 8. Sound and pacing

- **Layers**:
  1. Narrator: one dry, close, upfront voice throughout.
  2. Music bed: sub-bass-heavy instrumental at about 85 BPM, half-time feel, sparse percussion.

## 10. Why it works

1. **Borrowed-authority cold open.** It opens on a real, recognisable broadcast moment: the native graphic flips to P1 while a commentator shouts the name. There's no intro, logo or title, and the official F1 TV graphic is the title card.
2. **Word-synced kinetic captions.** The screen changes about 3 times a second on every spoken word, and the size hierarchy tells you which words matter. The 0.23 s blur-in makes each word feel like it glows into place, and it lands slightly ahead of the voice. That keeps it watchable on mute.
3. **A repeatable chapter template with built-in breaths.**
   - The 1-2-3 triptych promises a list and returns as the table of contents.
   - Each chapter runs F1 proof → paper punchline → startup example.
   - Music drop-outs and radio clips reset attention before each number.
   - The payoff line ("Behind every Verstappen is a Horner") turns the whole story into the brand pitch without a hard sell.
4. **One house look over chaotic sources.** The milky matte grade, one typeface, one paper colour and inversion flashes as the only "effect vocabulary" make 2005 broadcast TV, a Hollywood film and phone UI feel like one edit.

## 11. Re-using the structure (template)

Keep the structure and grammar, change the content:

1. **0–2 s cold open**: a real, iconic moment of the subject with diegetic audio that says their name. Leave the native graphic or text in. No music.
2. **First cut = music entry** with a pixel-mosaic dissolve into a hero photo plus the name blurring in.
3. **Thesis in about 25 s**: "X is the best ___ right now, and he'll be the first to tell you, he's not always ___, but he ___ anyway. Because ___ was never really about ___, it's about ___." Use paper cards, card collages, a strobe montage of photos (5 frames each), one archival audio sting, and at least one rolling counter.
4. **Two seconds of silence** with an ambient or archival line, then the **1-2-3 triptych**.
5. **Three chapters**, each with: number lockup, a story beat with name lockups and a counter, a paper punchline, a parallel example (interview plus UI or props), and inversion flashes on the big cuts.
6. **Payoff**: "That's not luck..." on paper, then the emotional hero shot with the thesis lockup ("Behind every ___ is a ___"), then black words on paper for the closing line, then the logo build and a sonic tag.

**Production rules**:
- Captions are one word at a time, white Helvetica Neue Bold (#F7F4F7), tracking −4 %, soft shadow. They blur in over 7 frames, starting about 3 frames before the word.
- Emphasis words are 3–5× bigger. Lower-case most words.
- Black words on paper scale from 114 % to 100 % over 0.4 s.
- Cut on the word, not the beat. Average shot length is about 1 s.
- Inversion flash of 2–6 frames on about one cut in five.
- Grade everything to milky blacks (about 20/255) and rolled-off highlights.
